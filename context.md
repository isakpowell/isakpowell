# AIYEP 2026

Isak has applied for the Australia-Indonesia Youth Exchange Program (AIYEP) 2026. If

## What AIYEP is

AIYEP is a bilateral youth exchange program established in 1982, run by the
Australia-Indonesia Institute (AII) within the Australian Government's Department of
Foreign Affairs and Trade, delivered in partnership with Indonesia's Ministry of Youth
and Sport. Value Learning delivers the program on behalf of the AII.

Each year 21 Australian and 21 Indonesian delegates aged 21 to 25 are selected. The
program links young people from both countries through social, professional and cultural
exchange, with the aim of deepening mutual understanding and building lasting
people-to-people ties. Delegates complete work placements, language training, academic
workshops and cultural activities, and live with host families in each country.

Selection is competitive and includes an interview with a panel from DFAT, the
Australia-Indonesia Institute and Value Learning. Australian delegates receive a full
scholarship covering flights, accommodation, meals, visas and insurance.

## Structure of the 2026 cycle

- Online component: approximately 4–6 hours per week over five weeks, including the AFS
  Global Competence Certificate
- Indonesian delegates spend four weeks in Melbourne, Australia
- In-person orientation: 1–4 December 2026 (tentative)
- In-country program in Indonesia: 12 January – 9 February 2027
- Post-program debrief follows the in-country phase

Indonesian language skills are not a prerequisite, but applicants are expected to show a
commitment to learning the basics before the program begins. Isak is learning Indonesian
at a beginner level.

## Why Isak applied

Three reasons, in his own framing.

**A professional connection that already exists.** Advance Chemicals manufactures
commercial laundry and sanitation chemicals and already exports into Indonesia. Isak is
being trained to take over a business whose future is tied to the region, and would
rather build those relationships at the start of his career than try to manufacture them
later.

**A different kind of travel.** Isak has travelled widely but always briefly. AIYEP is
built around living with a host family and working inside one organisation, which is the
extended, everyday immersion he has been looking for. His favourite part of his last
Indonesian trip was staying with a family in Karimunjawa rather than anything on an
itinerary.

**Family precedent, but his own reasons.** His sister Bronte completed AIYEP and remains
in regular contact with her host mother and Indonesian buddy years later. Isak saw what
the program produced for her, but applies for reasons of his own.

He applied for AIYEP 2025 and was not selected. What has changed since is that he is no
longer a student writing about a country in the abstract: he now has customers there and
operational responsibility for a business that depends on the relationship between the
two countries.
